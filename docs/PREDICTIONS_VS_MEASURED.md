# What the prior state of the art would have predicted, and what happened

Generated by `scripts/make_prediction_ledger.py`. Each row states the prediction that a prior method's own framing implies for this benchmark, then the measured outcome. Predictions are our reading of the prior work; the numbers are from `results/*.jsonl`.

Convention throughout: **energy score, lower is better**; percentages are paired-by-seed differences over 10 seeds with the identical backbone, training budget and data; $t$ is the paired statistic on 9 dof (|t| > 2.262 is significant at 5%).

## 1. Physics-Constrained Flow Matching (PCFM, NeurIPS 2025)

**Their position.** Hard constraints can be imposed at inference time on a pretrained flow, zero-shot, for arbitrary nonlinear constraints. The implied prediction: you never need to touch training, so an inference-time corrector should match or beat train-time constraint handling, at negligible cost.

**Predicted for SOTA:** PCFM $\approx$ or $<$ every train-time route on fidelity, at no meaningful overhead.

**Measured.**

* `grid` (codim 0.648): PCFM is +1.47% vs unconstrained FM ($t=+10.28$, significant) and train-time projection beats it by 3.39% ($t=-9.09$, significant). **Prediction fails here.**
* `measured` (codim 0.304): PCFM is +0.32% vs FM ($t=+9.34$) while train-time projection is +3.38% *worse* than PCFM ($t=+5.05$). **Prediction holds here** -- and holds decisively.
* Cost: PCFM inserts 51 extra projections into a 50-step solve; the projector route adds zero NFE and one cached matvec.

**Verdict.** PCFM's claim is suite-dependent, and neither we nor they could have known which regime applies without measuring codimension first. This is the paper's point, and it is a correction to both positions, not a defeat of theirs.

## 2. DC3 (Donti, Rolnick & Kolter, ICLR 2021) and reduced/nullspace coordinates

**Their position.** Enforce equality constraints by *completion* -- predict the free variables, solve for the rest -- so the network never wastes capacity on determined directions and constraint satisfaction is structural.

**Predicted for SOTA:** completion-based routes dominate, uniformly and by more as the number of determined variables grows.

**Measured.**

* `grid_noflow` (codim 0.093): DC3 is -2.82% ($t=-6.40$) and ranks **1 of 16** -- the best method on that suite.
* `measured` (codim 0.304): DC3 is +7.65% ($t=+16.20$) -- the *worst* of the five exact routes.
* `grid` (codim 0.648): DC3 is +0.80% ($t=+1.90$, not significant).

**Verdict.** The prediction is exactly backwards in its monotonicity: completion does best where there is *least* to complete, and worst in the middle. Feasibility is structural for DC3 everywhere, as claimed; fidelity is not.

## 3. Soft physics penalties (PINN-style; the default in physics-informed generation)

**Their position.** Add $\lambda\|Ax-b\|^2$ to the loss; raise $\lambda$ until violations are acceptable.

**Predicted for SOTA:** a tunable trade-off -- larger $\lambda$ buys feasibility at some fidelity cost, and a good $\lambda$ gets most of both.

**Measured on `grid`:**

| $\lambda$ | ES | rel. to FM | $\|Ax-b\|_\infty$ (MW) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 545.5 | +0.3% | 219 |
| 10 | 595.5 | +9.5% | 236 |
| 100 | 843.9 | +55.1% | 277 |
| 1000 | 1043 | +91.8% | 187 |

**Verdict. There is no trade-off to tune.** Three orders of magnitude of $\lambda$ moves the violation from 219 MW to 187 MW -- the same order, still operationally unacceptable -- while the energy score degrades monotonically to +92%. Every exact route dominates the entire penalty family on both axes simultaneously. This is the clearest negative result in the paper and the one with the most immediate practical consequence.

## 4. The power-systems scenario-generation literature (GAN/VAE-based)

**Their position.** Deep generative scenario generation supersedes classical resampling and copula methods.

**Predicted for SOTA:** cWGAN-GP / cVAE / normalising flows beat nearest-neighbour resampling and Gaussian copulas.

**Measured.**

| suite | kNN-Historical rank | cWGAN-GP | cVAE | NormFlow-RealNVP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `grid_noflow` | **2 of 16** | 13 | 10 | 16 |
| `measured` | **11 of 16** | 14 | 12 | 15 |
| `grid` | **2 of 16** | 10 | 11 | 13 |

**Verdict.** kNN-Historical -- resample analogue days, zero parameters, zero NFE, exactly feasible by construction -- places second of sixteen on both grid suites and beats every GAN, VAE and normalising flow on all three. Per the pre-registered commitment (C9), this goes in the abstract rather than a footnote.

## 5. The evaluation convention: proper scoring rules as a proxy for value

**The field's position.** Energy score and variogram score are proper, so a better score means a better scenario set; downstream evaluation is a nicety.

**Predicted for SOTA:** energy score ranks generators the way the operational decision would; constraint-exact scenario sets, being physically admissible, produce better dispatch decisions.

**Measured** on two-stage stochastic unit commitment, 16 generators, 3 seeds:

* corr(cost regret, energy score) = **+0.315** (Spearman $\rho=+0.229$, $p=0.393$) -- the right sign, but weak and **not statistically significant**.
* corr(cost regret, 90% coverage) = **-0.933** (Spearman $\rho=-0.618$, $p=0.011$).
* The three exactly-feasible train-time routes take the worst regret among neural methods (`FM+reduced` 65.6%, `FM+DC3` 58.5%, `HFM (ours)` 53.3%); the best are the two most over-dispersed baselines (`GaussianCopula` 16.5%, `NormFlow-RealNVP` 16.5%).

**Verdict, and the result we least expected.** Physical feasibility of the scenario set does not propagate to the scheduling decision at all; calibration does. Exactly-feasible ensembles are sharper, the unit-commitment model trusts them, under-commits reserve, and pays in load shed. A paper that had stopped at the energy score would have reported the opposite conclusion with confidence. (Stated with its limit: 3 seeds on this stage, not 10.)

## 6. Adaptive ODE sampling (`Gotta Go Fast`, dopri5)

**Their position.** Adaptive step-size solvers give the best accuracy-per-evaluation for generative ODEs.

**Predicted for SOTA:** `dopri5` at matched tolerance is the frontier; a fixed 20-step solve cannot match a 500-evaluation adaptive solve.

**Measured** (`grid`): unconstrained FM's best score anywhere on the frontier is 543.7 at NFE 548 (`dopri5`). Train-time projection reaches 539.5 at **NFE 20** (`euler`) -- 27x fewer function evaluations for a better score, with the violation at 0.000534 MW against 457 MW.

**Verdict.** The prediction is right about solvers and beside the point about this problem. Removing the determined directions from the hypothesis class buys far more than any solver schedule does, because the adaptive solver is spending its budget integrating directions that are known in closed form. We claim no novelty for adaptivity and report NFE and FLOPs rather than device joules.

## 7. Our own pre-registered predictions

Both were written into `docs/CLAIMS_AND_EVIDENCE.md` before the sweep ran, with explicit falsification conditions. Both fired.

| # | Pre-registered prediction | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| C3 | train-time $\le$ inference-time $\le$ post-hoc on fidelity, universally | **Falsified.** The ordering holds on `grid` (-3.39% for train-time vs inference-time, $t=-9.09$) and reverses on `measured` (+3.38%, $t=+5.05$). The reversal is the finding. |
| C5' | under an N-1 topology swap, physical-coordinate routes keep their learned distribution, chart-based routes do not | **Falsified.** `HFM` 896.4 vs `FM+reduced` 881.8 -- indistinguishable, and both beaten by `FM+posthoc` 651.5. Differentiator dropped, per the pre-registration. |

## 8. Our own nonlinear extension

**We predicted** that per-step retraction would be needed to bound drift on the AC manifold, as the geometric-integration literature suggests.

**Measured.** A single retraction after the solve reaches $|g|_\infty=0.0077$ from 5.1, essentially free in score. Per-step retraction at the default budget of 3 Gauss-Newton iterations reaches only 129 -- worse than doing nothing -- because the in-loop pull-back never converges and injects a biased correction at every step. Reported as a negative result with the diagnosed cause, not omitted.
