Where Does a Physical Constraint Belong in a Generative Model?

Constraint-exact scenario generation for power grids

Anonymous Authors
Under review at ICLR
Code Results Pre-registration

Abstract

Generative scenario models for power grids must obey the physics they describe — a set of injections that breaks Kirchhoff's laws is not a pessimistic forecast, it is an impossible one. Several methods achieve exact constraint satisfaction. The open question is where the constraint belongs: in the hypothesis class, at inference, in the loss, or in the coordinates.

We benchmark all four routes under identical backbones, budgets and data — 16 methods, 10 seeds, three suites built from real grid measurements — varying one thing: the codimension of the constraint set, the fraction of the state the physics already determines. The answer is not universal. On a controlled contrast, train-time projection moves from indistinguishable from doing nothing to a significant win; on a third real dataset the same code is significantly worse.

The two grid suites are the same 118-bus system and the same measured injections; the only change is whether the model must also emit the 186 line flows the constraint matrix determines exactly. That makes the contrast causal. Codimension goes 0.093 → 0.648 and train-time projection goes from indistinguishable to significantly better.

Anyone reporting a single winner has measured one suite.

Scenario feasibility does not reach the scheduling decision; calibration carries it. The exactly-feasible routes take the worst regret among neural methods (ours 53%, FM+reduced 66%); the best belong to the most over-dispersed baselines (16%). Exact ensembles are sharper, the scheduler trusts them, under-commits reserve and pays in load shed.

A study that stopped at the energy score would have reported the opposite. Limit: 3 seeds on this stage, not 10.

What We Predicted, and Got Wrong

Every claim was pre-registered with an explicit falsification condition before the sweep ran. Two fired.

Falsified · C3

Predicted: train-time ≤ inference-time ≤ post-hoc, universally.

Measured: holds on grid (-3.39%, t = -9.09), reverses on measured (+3.38%, t = +5.05). The reversal became the paper's central finding.

Falsified · C5′

Predicted: under an N-1 topology swap, physical-coordinate routes keep their learned distribution; chart-based routes do not.

Measured: ours 896.4 vs FM+reduced 881.8 — indistinguishable, both beaten by FM+posthoc 651.5. Differentiator dropped.

Negative result · AC manifold

Predicted: per-step retraction is needed to bound drift on the nonlinear AC manifold.

Measured: one retraction after the solve reaches |g|∞ = 0.0077 from 5.1, free in score. Per-step retraction at 3 Gauss-Newton iterations reaches only 129 — worse than doing nothing.

A non-neural baseline places second of sixteen on both grid suites. kNN-Historical resamples analogue days: zero parameters, zero NFE, exactly feasible by construction. It beats every GAN, VAE and normalising flow on all three suites.
